How the Algorithm Operates

Invented in 1854 by Charles Wheatstone and promoted by Lord Playfair, the Playfair cipher was the first practical digraphic substitution cipher used for tactical field communications.

  • 5×5 Matrix: 25 cells accommodate the 26-letter Latin alphabet by consolidating I and J into one cell. The keyword letters (duplicates removed) fill the start, followed by the unused alphabet letters in order.
  • Digraph Preparation: The plaintext is divided into two-letter pairs. If a pair contains identical letters (e.g., LL), a filler (usually X) is inserted between them. If the total length is odd, the trailing character is padded with the filler.
  • Three Geometric Rules:
    • Same Row: Shift each letter 1 position right (wrap around). Decrypt: 1 position left.
    • Same Column: Shift each letter 1 position down (wrap around). Decrypt: 1 position up.
    • Rectangle: Swap each letter with the one in its own row at the other letter's column. Decrypt: exactly the same swap!

Why Playfair Is Cryptographically Weak

While Playfair substantially outperforms single-letter monoalphabetic ciphers (e.g. Caesar, Affine), it remains vulnerable to modern and classical cryptanalysis:

  • Hides Single-Letter but NOT Digraph Frequencies: Instead of 26 single-letter frequencies, there are $25 \times 25 = 625$ (or $25 \times 24 = 600$ without identical pairs) digraphs. Common English bigrams such as TH, HE, IN, ER, AN, and RE produce noticeable frequency spikes in the ciphertext.
  • Symmetry & Reciprocity: If digraph AB encrypts to CD via the rectangle rule, then BA inevitably encrypts to DC, and CD decrypts to AB. This structural symmetry leaks plaintext relationships.
  • Self-Encryption Impossibility: A letter can never encrypt to itself ($E(x) \neq x$). This negative property leaks immense structural information during crib-dragging.
  • Known-Plaintext & Ciphertext-Only Attacks: With only a few dozen known digraph pairs, cryptanalysts can set up linear systems over the row and column coordinates to reconstruct the entire 5×5 key matrix without trying all keys.
